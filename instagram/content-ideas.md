# Kuleana Content Plan — Perils, Visuals, and What Other Firms Post

Three inputs shaped this plan: (1) the perils a Hawaii homeowner's policy
actually covers (and famously doesn't), (2) what established public adjusting
firms publish, and (3) the gaps in the current @yenclaims grid. Asset numbers
refer to files in `posts/` and `video/`.

---

## 1 · What other public adjusting firms publish

Recurring content patterns across established firms (Adjusters International,
large Florida/Texas PA firms, and PA marketing guides):

1. **Damage education** — "what this damage looks like / what it becomes."
   The single most common format. → Our posts 02, 08, 11–16, 18–20.
2. **Process explainers** — what a public adjuster does step by step, who the
   carrier's adjuster works for. → Posts 03, 04–07.
3. **Policy literacy** — deductibles, exclusions, endorsements, ACV vs RCV.
   Firms that do this well own the "read your policy" niche. → Posts 05–07, 17.
4. **Claim-story arcs** — before/during/after a claim (with client permission,
   no claim numbers). High trust value; we can only do this with real client
   consent, so it's a future format.
5. **Disaster-response posts** — timely posts after named storms with
   documentation checklists. → Posts 09, 14, 20 are the evergreen versions.
6. **Credential/trust posts** — licensing, bonding, regulator links, "how we
   get paid." Rare among competitors, big differentiator. → Post 01 + the
   license footer on every asset; a dedicated "how contingency fees work"
   carousel is a strong future post.
7. **What NOT to do** — competitor feeds that guarantee outcomes or flash
   settlement checks are compliance liabilities, not inspiration; Hawaii's
   adopted NAIC-model conduct rules and HRS §431:13-102 prohibit outcome
   promises. We deliberately avoid that lane (see `compliance.md`).

Format mix observed: static education tiles + short reels dominate; carousels
for multi-step topics; almost nobody brands consistently — which is exactly
where this set wins.
