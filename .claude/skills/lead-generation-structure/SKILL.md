# Lead Generation Structure

A general-purpose framework for turning a business — any business — into a
working lead-generation system. It was distilled from a real system built in
this repo for two licensed insurance-claims businesses (Kuleana Claim
Solutions and Monarch Claims Consultants), but every part below is written to
generalize: a contractor, a law practice, a financial advisor, a clinic, a
real-estate agent, or a SaaS product all need the same seven pieces, just
with different specifics filled in.

**Before inventing anything from scratch, read the worked example already in
this repo:** `instagram/captions.md`, `compliance.md`, `content-ideas.md`,
`dm-automation.md`, `schedule.md`, and `account-transformation-script.md` are
a complete, concrete instance of every pattern described below. It's faster
and safer to adapt a working example than to design in the abstract — use
the reference files here for the *method*, and that folder for a fully
fleshed-out *instance* of the method.

## Why these parts belong together

Leads come from trust, and trust compounds across a small number of
interlocking systems rather than any single silver-bullet tactic. A gorgeous
landing page with no content behind it converts nobody who hasn't heard of
the business yet; a great content system with no automation lets warm leads
go cold waiting for a reply; a perfect DM flow that promises outcomes it
can't legally back gets the business in regulatory trouble before it gets a
client. Build these as one system, in the order below, not as isolated
deliverables.

## 0 · Start with intake — always

Before writing a single line of copy, gather the business context: what it
sells, who buys it and why (especially *what triggers the search* — an
emergency, a recurring need, a life event), whether it's a regulated
profession, how it's paid, its service area, what accounts/assets already
exist, and what the best conversion channel actually is for this audience.
Skipping this produces generic marketing that could belong to any business —
which converts nobody. Read **`reference/intake.md`** and work through it
with the user (or make explicit, stated assumptions if this is an autonomous
task) before building any of the parts below.

## The seven parts

Not every business needs all seven on day one. Part 1 (landing page) and
Part 3 (content planning) are close to universal. Parts 2, 5, 6, and 7 are
situational — apply them when intake surfaces the trigger condition noted
for each.

| # | Part | Reference file | When it applies |
|---|------|----------------|------------------|
| 1 | Trust-first landing page | `reference/landing-page.md` | Nearly always — every business needs one place that converts a stranger into a call/booking/lead. |
| 2 | Compliance framework | `reference/compliance-framework.md` | Any regulated or trust-sensitive profession (licensing board, bar, medical board, financial regulator, insurance division, etc.) — do this **before** writing marketing copy, not after. |
| 3 | Content-planning method | `reference/content-planning.md` | Nearly always — anywhere the business needs a repeatable supply of content topics rather than one-off inspiration. |
| 4 | Caption / creative system | `reference/caption-system.md` | Once there's a content calendar to populate — keeps every asset recognizable and on-message. |
| 5 | DM/comment lead-capture automation | `reference/dm-automation.md` | Social platforms where prospects comment or DM instead of filling out a form (Instagram, TikTok, Facebook) — high-touch businesses especially. |
| 6 | Posting schedule / multi-brand queue | `reference/posting-schedule.md` | Once there's more than a couple of pieces of content queued, and especially once there's more than one brand/account to coordinate. |
| 7 | Dormant-account transformation | `reference/account-transformation.md` | Only when there's an existing, inactive account with an audience worth inheriting instead of starting a new account from zero. |

## Sequencing guidance

1. **Intake first**, always — it determines whether parts 2, 5, 6, and 7 even apply.
2. **Landing page + compliance pass together** if the business is regulated — the compliance checklist should shape the page copy from the first draft, not get bolted on after review.
3. **Content-planning method** before writing any actual captions — it produces the topic map that the caption system fills in, so sequencing it first avoids rewriting content once gaps show up.
4. **Caption system**, applying the content-planning output.
5. **DM automation**, only after there's live content actually asking people to comment/DM a keyword — never publish a caption that promises an automated response before the automation is built and tested end-to-end (see the setup checklist in `reference/dm-automation.md`).
6. **Posting schedule** once there's a backlog of ready and in-progress content to sequence.
7. **Account transformation** is a one-time, front-loaded project when it applies — run it before the other parts go live on that account, not alongside them.

## Principles that hold regardless of industry

- **A real human beats a faceless form for high-trust purchases.** If the
  business sells something people research anxiously (money, health, legal
  exposure, property damage), routing first contact to a phone call or a DM
  with a named person tends to out-convert a web form into a CRM queue. Ask
  which fits during intake — don't default to a form just because it's
  easiest to build.
- **Never promise what you can't control.** Outcomes, dollar amounts,
  timeframes, guarantees — describe the *process* the business runs, not the
  *result* the customer will get. This is both a compliance rule (see
  `reference/compliance-framework.md`) and honestly just good marketing:
  overpromising in an ad creates the exact customer complaints that sink a
  local business's reputation.
- **Consistency is the trust signal.** The same footer/signature block, the
  same palette, the same credential line, the same voice, on every single
  asset — a scattered feed reads as an unstable business even when the
  underlying work is excellent.
- **Disclose staged or synthetic content in the copy itself**, not just an
  on-image watermark — watermarks get cropped, captions don't. This applies
  to AI-generated imagery, stock photography, and re-created scenarios
  equally.
- **Log every conversation that turns into a real lead.** The first DM or
  comment thread is often the first record of the client relationship —
  automations should hand off to a human and disappear, never pretend to be
  the whole relationship.
- **One follow-up, then silence.** Automated sequences that keep pinging a
  non-responder read as spam and burn the account's reputation with the
  platform. A single, graceful follow-up that offers an easy out is the
  ceiling, not the floor, for automation persistence.
