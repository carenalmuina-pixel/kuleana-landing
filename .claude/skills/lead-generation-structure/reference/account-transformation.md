# Dormant-Account Transformation Playbook

Use this when intake (`reference/intake.md`, question 6) turns up an
existing account that's inactive but still has a real audience — converting
it into the new brand is often worth more than starting a fresh account
from zero, because it inherits followers, platform trust/age, and algorithm
history a new account has to earn all over again. This only applies when
there's a genuine audience-overlap case for it (see Phase 0) — a confused
rebrand can underperform a clean new account.

## Phase 0 — Decide before touching anything

1. Name the new brand: its handle, one-line promise, and target audience.
2. **Run the audience-overlap test, honestly.** Does the dormant account's
   existing audience share an identity or interest with the new brand's
   target customer (same geography, same life stage, same community, an
   adjacent interest)? If the overlap is real, the transformation is worth
   doing. If the overlap is near-zero (e.g., a sports-fan account being
   converted into a B2B insurance account), a fresh account will
   out-perform a confused rebrand — stop here and start clean instead.
3. **Decide per platform.** If the same owner has the dormant account on
   multiple platforms, each can transform independently — transform only
   where the audience genuinely fits, and leave the rest dormant rather than
   forcing a rebrand everywhere at once.

## Phase 1 — Audit and preserve (Day 0)

- Export the account's own data via the platform's official data-download
  tool before changing anything.
- Screenshot the current analytics: follower count, demographics (age,
  location, active hours). The **active-hours data is worth preserving even
  though the content isn't** — audience habits tend to survive a rebrand
  even when the content pivots completely, and it should set the new
  posting schedule.
- Save all previously posted content locally.
- Decide, per past post: **archive** anything that actively fights the new
  positioning (archive rather than delete — it preserves the account's age
  and history), or **keep** anything that still fits the new brand.

## Phase 2 — Identity swap (Day 1, all in one sitting)

Do the entire identity swap within one sitting so the account is never
left half-branded in between:

1. Handle change (note any platform-specific cooldown/lock on reusing the
   old handle, and any limit on how often the handle can change).
2. Display name — put the actual keyword people would search for in the
   name field itself, not only in the brand word.
3. Profile photo and bio, including the new one-line promise and CTA link.
4. Rebuild Highlights/pinned content around the new pillars; unpin
   anything from the old identity.
5. Post the announcement (Phase 3) and pin it.

## Phase 3 — The announcement post

Post at the audience's historically peak active hour from the Phase 1
screenshots. A reliable script shape, adaptable to any pivot:

> **Hook (first few seconds):** "You followed me for [OLD THING]. Here's
> what's next."
> **Bridge:** "[OLD THING] was a chapter — and what I loved most about it
> was [the honest thread that connects it to what's next: the community,
> the craft, the people]. That's not ending. It's growing into something
> else."
> **Reveal:** "This account is now **[NEW BRAND]** — [one-line promise].
> Same [voice/face/energy], new mission: [what followers get, concretely,
> on an ongoing basis]."
> **CTA:** "If you're here for [NEW VALUE], stay — [first concrete thing]
> drops [when]. If not, no hard feelings — thank you for the ride."

Reply to every comment in the first 24 hours — the comment section on this
specific post is where the audience decides whether to stay.

## Phase 4 — The 30-day bridge

- **Cadence ramp:** roughly a third of the eventual full cadence for the
  first one to two weeks (don't flood a freshly repositioned audience),
  then ramp to the new brand's intended full cadence.
- **Rule of thirds for the first ~12 posts:** about a third familiar-format
  content applied to the new topic (keep the editing style/voice the
  audience already recognizes), a third pure new-brand value content, and a
  third identity/story content (why the pivot, who it's for now).
- Re-cut the best-performing old formats with new-topic content before
  trying brand-new formats — a proven hook structure transfers better than
  a first-time experiment.
- Expect and accept a follower dip (commonly in the 5-15% range) — watch
  saves, replies, and DMs rather than follower count as the real signal for
  the first month.

## Phase 5 — Measure and prune (Day 30)

- Compare reach/saves against the Phase 1 baseline screenshots.
- Keep the two or three best-performing new formats, drop the weakest, and
  fold the winners into the ongoing master posting queue
  (`reference/posting-schedule.md`).
