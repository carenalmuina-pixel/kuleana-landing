# Compliance Framework for Regulated or Trust-Sensitive Professions

Apply this whenever intake (`reference/intake.md`, question 3) turns up a
licensed or regulated profession — insurance (adjusting, agents, brokers),
legal, medical, financial/investment advice, contracting, real estate, and
similar fields. **This is a method for producing a first-draft-safe content
checklist, not legal advice.** Always say so explicitly in whatever
checklist or content you produce, and recommend the business have its own
counsel or compliance officer do a final read before anything goes out
publicly — the checklist de-risks the draft, it doesn't replace review.

## The method

1. **Identify the governing statute/rule set and the licensing body** for
   the profession, in the relevant jurisdiction. Note where a prospect can
   independently verify the license (a state licensee-search tool, a bar
   directory, etc.) — that verifiability is itself a trust signal worth
   surfacing on the landing page.

2. **Build a cross-reference table**: one row per legal requirement, mapped
   to a concrete content rule. The categories below recur across almost
   every regulated profession — treat them as a starting checklist and add
   anything specific to this jurisdiction/profession:

   - **Identification.** Full name, license number, and regulator must
     appear on marketing material, mirroring what's already required in the
     business's written contracts. Bake this into a footer/signature block
     used on every single asset so it's structurally impossible to forget.

   - **No misrepresentation.** Never promise outcomes, dollar amounts,
     percentages, or timeframes. Describe the *process* the business runs,
     not the *result* the customer will get. Use hedged, defensible language
     — "many," not "most" or "all"; "can be," not "will be." A denial isn't
     "always overturned," it's "a position, not a verdict."

   - **Imagery and content honesty.** Disclose staged, recreated, stock, or
     AI-generated imagery **in the caption/copy itself**, worded so the
     disclosure survives cropping or re-posting — an on-image watermark
     alone isn't enough, because it can be cropped off. Never present
     synthetic or stock imagery as a real client's property or case. If a
     real client's photo, story, or likeness is used, get written
     permission first and strip identifying details (case/file numbers,
     addresses, exact dates).

   - **Fee representation.** Fee or compensation terms belong in the
     written contract where the law requires them, not in an ad. Describe
     the fee *model* honestly (e.g., "no recovery, no fee," "flat fee,"
     "free consultation") without quoting specific percentages or dollar
     figures if the governing rules reserve those for the signed agreement.

   - **No unauthorized practice of an adjacent regulated profession.** A
     public adjuster shouldn't give legal advice; a bookkeeper shouldn't
     give tax advice unless separately licensed for it; a real-estate agent
     shouldn't give mortgage-underwriting guidance. Route those questions to
     the appropriately licensed professional rather than answering them —
     both in content and in any automated DM flow.

   - **Referral / financial-interest disclosure.** Disclose any paid
     referral relationship with a vendor or partner. The simplest safe
     default, if there's no strong reason otherwise, is not to endorse
     specific third-party vendors or contractors at all.

   - **Timing sensitivity.** Solicitation rules often tighten immediately
     after a declared disaster or emergency (storm, disaster declaration,
     accident cluster). Build an explicit pause switch into the content
     calendar for exactly this scenario — when in doubt, pause
     urgency-driven CTAs and keep only educational content live.

3. **Turn the table into a short, standing "things to keep doing" list** —
   the artifact everyone writing future copy can follow without re-deriving
   the law each time. This is what actually scales: a five-to-eight-item
   checklist that any future caption, ad, or DM script gets checked against
   before it ships.

4. **Cite sources and flag the review step.** List the statutes/rules
   consulted, and end with an explicit "have the business's own counsel or
   compliance officer do a final read before publishing" line. This isn't
   boilerplate CYA — professions and jurisdictions vary enough that a
   generalized checklist genuinely cannot substitute for a local expert's
   sign-off.

## Applying it to automation and captions

Every other part of this skill inherits these rules directly:
- `reference/dm-automation.md`'s scripted messages must pass the same
  no-misrepresentation and identification checks as any other ad copy.
- `reference/caption-system.md`'s signature block is where the
  identification requirement gets baked in structurally.
- `reference/content-planning.md`'s "what NOT to do" step exists
  specifically to keep competitor-inspired content from importing a
  compliance violation along with the format.
