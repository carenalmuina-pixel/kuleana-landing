# Posting Schedule / Multi-Brand Queue

Once there's more than a couple of pieces of content ready or in progress,
sequence them in one master queue — and especially once there's more than
one brand or account involved, because collisions across brands are exactly
what a per-account calendar can't catch.

## Structure

- **One master queue, even across multiple brands/accounts.** Columns: date,
  time (in the audience's local timezone — mark it explicitly if different
  brands serve different timezones), account/brand, the content item, and
  its source/status. A single shared queue is the only way to see "brand A
  and brand B are both posting about the same topic this week" before it
  happens.

- **Explicit asset status.** Track "ready to post" separately from "still a
  brief / needs production" — a queue that quietly conflates the two gives
  a false sense of how much is actually done. Make this visible at a
  glance.

- **Cohesion rules within a single account's grid.** Alternate visual
  treatments deliberately (photo tile vs. text tile, color rotation across
  a brand's palette) so the feed doesn't repeat itself; decide the
  alternation pattern once and check new items against it before
  scheduling.

- **Cross-brand offset.** If the same owner or team runs more than one
  account, never schedule the same topic on two accounts in the same
  short window — offset by several days at minimum, so the same idea
  doesn't look copy-pasted to anyone following both. This is easy to miss
  without the shared queue above.

- **Cadence ramp, not a jump.** A new or freshly-repurposed account should
  ramp posting frequency gradually (see
  `reference/account-transformation.md` for the specific ramp during a
  rebrand) rather than launching at full planned cadence immediately —
  flooding a small or newly-repositioned audience risks a wave of
  unfollows before the new content has proven itself.

## Pre-launch checklist

Before anything in the queue actually goes live:

1. The business owner (or whoever owns final sign-off) approves the queue
   itself, not just individual assets.
2. Compliance sign-off on captions and disclosures, per
   `reference/compliance-framework.md`, especially for anything newly
   produced rather than reused.
3. Contact details, license numbers, and links are confirmed correct in
   every footer/signature block — an error here propagates across every
   asset that reuses the template.
4. If a DM/comment automation is referenced in any caption
   (`reference/dm-automation.md`), confirm it has already been tested
   end-to-end — never let a queue publish a promise the automation can't
   yet keep.
