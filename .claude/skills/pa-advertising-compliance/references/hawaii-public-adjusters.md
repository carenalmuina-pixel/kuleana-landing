## Governing law

Hawaii Revised Statutes chapter 431, article 9 (the public-adjuster provisions Hawaii adopted in 2020 via SB2876 SD1, which follow the NAIC Public Adjuster Licensing Model Act), plus the Insurance Code's general advertising rules.

## What governs Hawaii PA advertising

**1. Identification — HRS §431:9-201 and the NAIC-model contract provisions.**
A license is required to act as an adjuster, and the public adjuster's full name, business address, and license number (as on record with the Insurance Division) must appear consistently — both in written contracts and, as a matter of consistent practice, in advertising. Keep the exact wording constant across assets: `HI Public Adjuster License #19246735 · Hawaiʻi DCCA Insurance Division · No recovery, no fee` is the format already in use — reuse it rather than inventing variants.

**2. No misrepresentation — HRS §431:13-102 and article 9's discipline provisions.**
Misrepresentation is both a general unfair-trade-practice violation and separate grounds for license discipline. In practice:
- No outcome, settlement-amount, or percentage-increase promises.
- Hedge claims about reopening denied claims ("many," never "most" or "all").
- Recreated/staged/AI damage imagery must be disclosed as such in the caption of every post using it — the disclosure is the compliance control, not an on-image stamp (which can be cropped off). Never present such imagery as an actual client property or claim file.
- Real client photos need the client's written permission and no claim numbers, addresses, or policy details visible.

**3. Fee representation — the adopted NAIC-model fee provisions.**
Compensation terms belong in the written contract. If the carrier pays or commits to policy limits within **72 hours of the loss report**, the public adjuster is limited to reasonable time-based compensation rather than the standard contingency fee — copy should never promise a fee arrangement to a specific claimant, and should never quote a percentage at all (percentages belong in the signed contract).

**4. No unauthorized practice of law.**
Route disputed-claim content toward negotiation, the policy's appraisal clause, or referral to coverage counsel — never present as legal representation or advice.

**5. Financial-interest / referral disclosure.**
Any paid referral relationship with a repair contractor or vendor needs disclosure. Simplest safe default: don't endorse specific third-party vendors in content at all.

**6. General advertising hygiene.**
- The "licensed public adjuster" claim must be accurate and independently verifiable via the DCCA Insurance Division licensee search.
- "Free inspection"/"free policy review" language must match services actually offered free.
- Framing that contrasts the insurer's adjuster with the insured's own adjuster ("two different adjusters, two different clients") must stay factual, not disparaging of any named insurer (ties back to §431:13-102's defamation/false-statement rules).

**7. Enforcement.** Hawaii can fine unlicensed or non-compliant public-adjuster advertising **$1,000–$10,000 per violation** — a different, higher range than Florida's. Don't quote Florida's $500–$5,000 figure for Hawaii content.

## Checklist — every Kuleana asset before it ships

1. License line present verbatim: `HI Public Adjuster License #19246735 · Hawaiʻi DCCA Insurance Division · No recovery, no fee` (or the contract-matching equivalent for non-social formats).
2. No outcome/amount/timeframe promises; hedged language only.
3. Staged/AI/recreated imagery disclosed in the caption; real client photos have written permission and stripped identifiers.
4. No fee percentages quoted anywhere in the ad.
5. No implication of legal representation or legal advice for disputed claims.
6. No undisclosed vendor/contractor referral relationships.
7. "Licensed" claim and any "free X" claim are both independently verifiable/accurate as written.
8. Insurer-contrast framing (if used) is factual, not disparaging of a named carrier.
