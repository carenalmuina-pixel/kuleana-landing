# Florida Public Adjuster Advertising — Statute Reference

For JLAC Corp content (subcontractor appointed by Monarch Claims Consultants), Lic. #W583930, `@yenclaims`. Verified via fresh search 2026-08-05 — this is not carried over from training data, which is exactly why the correction below matters.

## Corrected: there is no 48-hour post-catastrophe solicitation ban

Florida Statute §626.854(6) (2008) once barred public adjusters from initiating contact or soliciting an insured within 48 hours of a loss event. **The Florida Supreme Court affirmed this was unconstitutional in 2012** (the First District's ruling, upheld on appeal) — the court found it barred all communication, not just the aggressive kind it was meant to stop, and the state couldn't show it was narrowly tailored. It has not been reinstated. If any prior note in this account, or any draft compliance copy, references a "48-hour rule" for Florida, it's referencing dead law — don't carry it forward.

Sources: Florida Supreme Court ruling coverage via Insurance Journal (Dec. 2010 reporting on the initial ruling), Merlin Law Group and Radey Law Firm (2012 coverage of the Supreme Court affirmance), *Property Insurance Coverage Law Blog*.

## What actually governs Florida PA advertising

**1. Identification — §626.8732.**
Every advertisement, in any format, must include the full name and license number (as it appears in Department of Financial Services records) of the public adjuster causing it to appear. If a firm with multiple licensed adjusters runs the ad, it needs the full name and license number of the firm's designated primary adjuster. This is the Florida equivalent of Hawaii's license-footer requirement — bake it into every asset template the same way.

**2. Solicitation timing — §626.854.**
Solicitation of an insured or claimant, by any means, is restricted to Monday through Saturday, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. This is a real, live restriction — unlike the defunct 48-hour rule above, this one is current. It applies to direct outreach (DMs, calls) more than to passive feed content, but scheduled posts with a direct solicitation CTA should still respect it if the platform/tooling makes that practical.

**3. Contract cancellation windows — §626.854.**
- Standard: the insured/claimant can cancel a PA contract without penalty within **10 days** of signing.
- During a Governor-declared state of emergency, and for **1 year after the date of loss**: the cancellation window shortens to **5 business days** after the contract is executed.
This matters for any copy describing the engagement process — don't imply the contract is locked in immediately upon signing.

**4. No misrepresentation / deceptive advertising — §626.9541.**
It's an unfair and deceptive trade practice to circulate any advertisement containing an untrue, deceptive, or misleading assertion about the business of insurance. Specifically called out:
- Inviting a policyholder to submit a claim when they don't actually have covered damage is deceptive — don't write copy that fishes for claims regardless of whether damage exists.
- No guarantees of specific claim outcomes.
- No implying the adjuster has authority over the insurer's payment decision (the adjuster represents the insured, not the carrier — this is the same "two adjusters, two clients" framing already used in Kuleana's content and works equally well for Monarch).
- No material misrepresentation intended to effect a settlement on less favorable terms than the policy actually provides.

**5. Enforcement.** Florida DFS investigates complaints and can fine **$500–$5,000 per violation**, scaled to severity. This is a different range than Hawaii's — don't quote Hawaii's $1,000–$10,000 figure for Florida content.

## Checklist — every Monarch/JLAC Corp asset before it ships

1. Full name + license #W583930 (or the firm's designated primary adjuster, if that's who's actually running the ad) visible on the asset itself, not just the linked site.
2. No claim-outcome, dollar-amount, or percentage promises. Hedged language only ("many," "can be," not "most/all/will").
3. No copy inviting claims regardless of actual damage — description of service, not a fishing hook.
4. No implication the adjuster controls or influences the insurer's payment decision.
5. Staged/recreated/AI imagery disclosed in the caption itself; real client photos have written permission and stripped identifiers.
6. No fee percentages or dollar figures quoted in the ad — fee model description only ("no recovery, no fee").
7. Any direct-solicitation CTA (DM prompts, "call now") is schedulable/deliverable within Mon–Sat 8am–8pm if the platform allows control over that.
8. If there's currently a Governor-declared state of emergency active in Florida: don't imply a locked-in contract before mentioning the 5-business-day cancellation window if the copy describes signing terms.
